Self-hosted n8n or n8n Cloud: what automation really costs a small business

For a small company running a dozen automations, n8n Cloud is almost always cheaper than running your own server once you count everything, not just the hosting invoice. Self-hosting wins in three situations: high volume, strict rules about where data lives, or access to systems that never leave your office. And only if someone owns the server.

What n8n Cloud costs

n8n charges per execution, meaning one full run of a workflow, not per step. A 40-step workflow costs the same as a two-step one.

Prices exclude VAT and reflect n8n's pricing in autumn 2026. Data on the hosted plans is stored in the EU, on servers in Frankfurt, and n8n offers a data processing agreement with the EU standard contractual clauses.

What self-hosting costs on paper

The software itself is free. The Community Edition runs under the Sustainable Use License, costs nothing for internal business use and has no cap on executions.

You pay for the server, and 2026 has quietly broken the old "€4 VPS" story. Hetzner, long the cheapest European option, raised prices more than once this year. In June, one popular mid-range instance more than doubled, and the cheapest tiers are often simply unavailable to order. A realistic budget for a server that comfortably runs n8n with a PostgreSQL database is €6–20 a month, plus a couple of euros for backup storage.

That puts self-hosting at roughly €10–20 a month against €50–60 for Cloud Pro. It looks like an easy saving. It isn't the whole bill.

The line items nobody quotes you

Upgrades. n8n moves fast and ships often. Someone has to install the updates, read the release notes, and check that your workflows still behave the same afterwards.

Backups you have actually restored from. A backup you've never tested is a hope, not a backup.

The encryption key. Every password and token n8n stores for your integrations is encrypted with a single key. Lose it during a server move, and you reconnect every integration by hand.

The database. Out of the box n8n uses SQLite. Production work needs PostgreSQL, which is one more service to configure, update and back up.

Security. Operating system patches, HTTPS certificates, closed ports. A server running n8n holds the keys to all your other systems at once, which makes it an attractive target.

The person who answers when it stops. The queue stalls on a Friday evening and invoices don't go out. Who notices, and who fixes it?

In our estimate, careful upkeep of a small n8n server takes 2–4 hours a month when things are quiet, and a lot more in the month something breaks. At €40 an hour, that's €80–160 a month — more than Cloud Pro.

A worked example: a five-person company

Say you run 8 automations and about 6,000 executions a month.

Self-hosting starts to pay off when you're running tens of thousands of executions a month and Cloud limits start to bite, or when upkeep costs you nothing extra because someone already looks after your servers.

AI model costs (OpenAI, Anthropic and others) sit on top in either case. For a small business that's typically €10–50 a month.

When self-hosting genuinely makes sense

  • Volume. Tens of thousands of executions a month, especially frequent, short workflows.
  • Data. Your contracts or your clients forbid sending data to an outside service, even one hosted in the EU. n8n Cloud stores data in Frankfurt but runs on a US provider's infrastructure, and for some clients that matters.
  • Internal systems. n8n needs to reach an accounting or ERP system that sits in your office and isn't exposed to the internet.
  • You already have IT. If someone already maintains your servers, one more is a small addition.

The licence matters if a contractor builds your automations

n8n's free licence allows internal business use and allows contractors to build workflows for clients. What it doesn't allow, per the licence FAQ, is hosting n8n as a service where clients build their own workflows. The line isn't about who sends the invoice. It's about who owns the n8n instance and who can edit what runs on it.

The cleanest setup: you own the instance — your Cloud account or your server — and the contractor builds inside it. If you ever change contractors, nothing leaves with them: the workflows, the connections and the history stay with you.

Our rule

Self-host when volume or data requirements make it necessary, and only when someone on the client side owns the server in writing. Otherwise, managed hosting costs less than your first incident.

Choosing in five minutes

  1. Count how many executions your workflows run a month, or will run.
  2. Check whether your clients or contracts set rules on where data is stored.
  3. Answer honestly: who updates the server, and who fixes it on a Friday evening?
  4. If question three has no name next to it, choose Cloud.
